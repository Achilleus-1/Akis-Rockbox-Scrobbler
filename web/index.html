A FEW SIMPLE STEPS

Keep the record.

From offline listening to your Last.fm library.

01 · Get your listening log

On older Rockbox versions, enable Last.fm logging. On current builds, enable playback logging, then open Plugins → Applications → lastfm_scrobbler and export the playback log. This app treats every entry as at least 50% listened, including entries Rockbox marks skipped.

Connect your player over USB. Find .scrobbler.log in its root folder. You can import it directly from the player or copy it to your PC first. Raw playback.log stores paths instead of song metadata and must be exported on the player first.

02 · Set up Last.fm once

Open Settings and follow the link to create a Last.fm API account. Name the application “Aki's Rockbox Scrobbler”, describe it as your personal Rockbox log importer, and leave optional callback/website fields blank. Paste the API key and shared secret into Settings.

Click Connect Last.fm, approve access on Last.fm’s website, then return here and click “I’ve authorized access”. Your Last.fm password is never entered in this app.

03 · Review and send

Import the log, check the artist, title, and listening times, and select the plays you want to send. Confirm the account and selection. Submissions use the dates shown in your queue, in batches of up to 50.

About times and duplicates

Every entry is treated as at least 50% listened, including skipped entries and short songs. A two-week submission cutoff applies by default. Older entries stay in the queue as “Too old”. Enable “Adjust outdated listening dates” in Settings to spread them across the last 13 days, preserving their order. Original dates remain saved locally; Last.fm displays the adjusted submission dates. Last.fm may still ignore entries under its own server rules; the app shows those results. Missing metadata, invalid durations or timestamps, future times, malformed lines, and exact duplicates are excluded with an explanation. The exported format does not contain elapsed listening time; 50% is your chosen assumption, not a measurement.

Unix timestamps are treated as UTC and displayed in your PC’s local time zone. If your player writes local clock time as UTC, you can explicitly apply a time correction before importing. For a player set to UTC−5, subtract 5 hours; use the offset at the time you listened, including daylight saving time. No times are invented for players without a working clock.

Accepted plays are remembered separately for each Last.fm account. Matching artist, title, and timestamp prevents resubmitting from this app. Plays submitted through other apps are not checked against your remote history.

If a submission is interrupted

A request may reach Last.fm even if its reply never reaches your PC. These plays are labeled “Uncertain” and held back. Check your Last.fm library, then use “Check outcome” to mark a play as received or allow a retry. A retry can duplicate a play if it was already accepted.

SMALL APP. CLEAR BOUNDARIES.

Your plays. Your PC.

A straightforward tool you can inspect, without a cloud account.

Only your PC and Last.fm

The interface runs on 127.0.0.1, accessible only on this PC. No analytics, ads, remote fonts, or third-party scripts. The app’s network requests go directly to Last.fm’s official API over HTTPS. Your browser visits Last.fm when you sign in or open a profile.

You choose what gets sent

Importing saves song metadata in the local queue. Nothing is submitted until you select plays and confirm. The app sends artist, title, album, listening time, duration, and optional track number and MusicBrainz ID. It never edits or deletes your source log or music files.

Your account credentials

Sign-in happens on Last.fm. The app receives an authorization session key, never your password. By default, credentials stay in memory for this run. If you select “Remember on this PC”, Windows DPAPI encrypts the API key, shared secret, and session key for your Windows user. This protects saved credentials, but software running as your Windows user may still access them.

Your local storage

Queue metadata and submission receipts are stored in an unencrypted SQLite database here:

Clear queue removes the imported track metadata. Submission fingerprints remain to prevent repeats. To remove all app data, stop the app and delete this folder. Keep it if you want duplicate protection to survive reinstalls.

Disconnecting

Disconnect removes the app’s saved credentials and active session. To revoke access on Last.fm as well, remove Aki's Rockbox Scrobbler from Last.fm’s application settings.

Readable source, no package installs

This app uses Python’s standard library and local HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It requires Python 3.10 or newer and a modern browser. It has been tested locally; it has not received an independent security audit. Close the terminal when you’re done to stop its local server.
